Unit 3 – Refrigeration and Air Conditioning
Introduction, classification and types of refrigeration systems and air-conditioning; applications of refrigeration and air-conditioning

1. Introduction to Refrigeration

1.1 Meaning

Refrigeration is the process of maintaining a region at a temperature lower than its surroundings by removing heat from it. In simple words, it is the science of producing and maintaining low temperatures.

The unit of refrigeration is commonly expressed in tonnes of refrigeration (TR), where 1 TR is the rate of heat removal required to freeze 1 tonne of water at 0°C in 24 hours.

1.2 Basic idea of a refrigeration system

A refrigeration system absorbs heat from a low‑temperature space (refrigerated space) and rejects it to a higher‑temperature environment (surroundings) with the help of external work, usually supplied by an electric motor.

2. Classification and Types of Refrigeration Systems

2.1 Based on working principle

  • Vapour compression refrigeration system (VCRS): Most widely used system in domestic and industrial applications.
  • Vapour absorption refrigeration system (VARS): Uses heat energy instead of mechanical work for compression.
  • Gas refrigeration system: Uses gas as working fluid, mainly for aircraft and special applications.

2.2 Vapour compression refrigeration system (simple idea)

The basic components are compressor, condenser, expansion device and evaporator. The refrigerant circulates through these components in a closed cycle.

  • Compressor: Raises the pressure and temperature of refrigerant vapour.
  • Condenser: Rejects heat to surroundings and condenses vapour into liquid.
  • Expansion valve: Reduces pressure and temperature of liquid refrigerant.
  • Evaporator: Absorbs heat from the refrigerated space and evaporates the refrigerant.

3. Introduction to Air Conditioning

3.1 Meaning

Air conditioning is the process of treating air to control its temperature, humidity, cleanliness and distribution to provide comfort or meet industrial requirements.

Comfort air conditioning aims to maintain conditions suitable for human comfort, while industrial air conditioning maintains conditions required for specific processes or products.

3.2 Elements of air conditioning

  • Temperature control: Heating or cooling of air.
  • Humidity control: Humidification or dehumidification.
  • Air cleanliness: Filtration and removal of dust and contaminants.
  • Air movement: Proper circulation and distribution of air.

4. Classification and Types of Air Conditioning Systems

4.1 Based on purpose

  • Comfort air conditioning: For residential, office, commercial buildings.
  • Industrial air conditioning: For textile mills, printing, electronic industries, etc.

4.2 Based on season

  • Summer air conditioning: Mainly cooling and dehumidification.
  • Winter air conditioning: Heating and humidification.
  • Year‑round air conditioning: Both heating and cooling as required.

4.3 Based on equipment arrangement

  • Window air conditioner
  • Split air conditioner
  • Central air conditioning system

5. Applications of Refrigeration and Air Conditioning

5.1 Applications of refrigeration

  • Preservation of food in domestic refrigerators and cold storages.
  • Ice plants and dairy industry.
  • Refrigerated transport of perishable goods.
  • Process cooling in chemical and pharmaceutical industries.
  • Low‑temperature research and testing.

5.2 Applications of air conditioning

  • Comfort in homes, offices, theatres, malls and vehicles.
  • Control of temperature and humidity in textile, paper and printing industries.
  • Clean rooms for electronics, pharmaceutical and medical applications.
  • Data centres and server rooms for reliable operation of equipment.
