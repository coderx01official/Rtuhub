1. Introduction to Mechanical Engineering

1.1 Meaning and scope

Mechanical engineering is the branch of engineering that deals with machines, mechanisms and energy systems. It focuses on how forces, motion, heat and materials interact, and how these can be used to design useful devices and processes.

The work of a mechanical engineer ranges from designing small components like bolts and gears to large systems such as power plants, vehicles and industrial production lines. The aim is always to achieve reliable performance with reasonable cost and safety.

1.2 Core areas of mechanical engineering

Important core areas are:

  • Thermal engineering: Study of heat, work and energy conversion.
  • Design of machines: Deciding the size, shape and material of machine parts.
  • Manufacturing technology: Processes used to convert raw material into finished products.
  • Industrial engineering: Improving productivity and efficiency of industrial systems.

1.3 Role of a mechanical engineer

  • Analyse problems related to energy, motion and forces.
  • Design machines and systems to perform specific tasks.
  • Select suitable materials and manufacturing processes.
  • Ensure safety, reliability and maintainability of equipment.
  • Work with other branches such as electrical, civil and electronics in multidisciplinary projects.

2. Concepts of Thermal Engineering

2.1 Basic ideas

Thermal engineering deals with heat, temperature and energy conversion. It explains how heat is produced, how it flows and how it can be converted into useful work in engines and turbines.

The subject mainly includes thermodynamics, heat transfer and thermal systems such as engines, boilers, refrigerators and air‑conditioners.

2.2 Thermodynamic systems and properties

A system is the part of the universe chosen for study, for example a gas in a cylinder. Everything outside it is called the surroundings. The boundary separates the system from the surroundings.

  • Closed system: Mass remains constant, energy can cross the boundary.
  • Open system: Both mass and energy can cross the boundary (e.g., turbine, compressor).
  • Isolated system: Neither mass nor energy crosses the boundary.

Important properties are pressure, temperature, volume, internal energy and enthalpy.

2.3 First and second law (simple view)

  • First law: Energy is conserved. Heat supplied to a system is partly stored and partly converted into work.
  • Second law: Gives the direction of processes. Heat flows naturally from hot body to cold body, not the reverse.

2.4 Modes of heat transfer

  • Conduction: Heat transfer through solids due to temperature difference.
  • Convection: Heat transfer between a surface and a moving fluid.
  • Radiation: Heat transfer by electromagnetic waves, even through vacuum.

2.5 Heat engines and refrigerators

A heat engine is a device that converts heat energy into mechanical work by operating in a cycle. Petrol engines, diesel engines, steam engines and gas turbines are examples.

A refrigerator transfers heat from a low temperature region to a high temperature region by consuming external work. It is used for cooling and preservation of food and other materials.

3. Mechanical Machine Design

3.1 Meaning of machine design

Machine design is the process of deciding the size, shape and material of machine parts so that they can safely carry the required loads and perform the desired function. It converts a need or idea into a practical machine.

A good design should be safe, reliable, easy to manufacture and economical. It should also consider maintenance and expected life of the machine.

3.2 Steps in design

  • Understand the purpose and working conditions of the machine.
  • Identify all forces and loads acting on each part.
  • Select suitable materials based on strength, toughness, hardness and cost.
  • Calculate stresses and choose a proper factor of safety.
  • Fix dimensions of parts and check for deflection, wear and fatigue.
  • Choose suitable manufacturing processes and tolerances.

3.3 Common machine elements

  • Shafts: Used to transmit torque and power between components.
  • Gears: Used to transmit motion and power with a definite speed ratio.
  • Bearings: Support rotating shafts and reduce friction.
  • Springs: Store energy and absorb shocks or vibrations.
  • Couplings and clutches: Connect and disconnect shafts when required.

4. Industrial Engineering

4.1 Meaning and purpose

Industrial engineering focuses on improving the performance of industrial systems by making the best possible use of men, machines, materials and methods. The aim is to achieve maximum output with minimum input, without compromising quality or safety.

It deals with planning, designing and controlling production systems so that work flows smoothly, resources are used efficiently and costs remain under control.

4.2 Key functions of industrial engineering

  • Work study: Improving the method of doing a job and determining the standard time.
  • Production planning and control (PPC): Ensuring that production takes place as per schedule.
  • Inventory control: Maintaining optimum stock levels to avoid shortages and excess storage.
  • Quality control: Ensuring products meet required standards using inspection and statistical tools.
  • Plant layout: Arranging machines and departments for smooth material flow.
  • Material handling: Selecting efficient methods for movement of materials.

4.3 Work study

Work study is divided into two major parts:

  • Method study: Examines how a job is done and finds a better method.
  • Time study: Determines the time required by a qualified worker to complete a job.

4.4 Production planning and control

PPC ensures that production takes place efficiently. It includes routing, scheduling, loading, dispatching and follow‑up.

5. Manufacturing Technology

5.1 Meaning

Manufacturing technology deals with the processes used to convert raw materials into finished products. The choice of process depends on the required shape, size, accuracy, material and production quantity.

5.2 Classification of manufacturing processes

  • Casting processes
  • Forming processes
  • Machining processes
  • Welding and joining processes
  • Powder metallurgy

5.3 Casting

Casting involves pouring molten metal into a mould cavity and allowing it to solidify. It is suitable for complex shapes and large components.

  • Pattern making
  • Moulding
  • Melting and pouring
  • Solidification
  • Cleaning and finishing

5.4 Forming processes

Forming changes the shape of material by plastic deformation without removing material.

  • Forging
  • Rolling
  • Extrusion
  • Drawing

5.5 Machining processes

Machining removes unwanted material in the form of chips using cutting tools.

  • Turning (lathe)
  • Milling
  • Drilling
  • Grinding

5.6 Welding and joining

Welding permanently joins two metal parts using heat, pressure or both.

5.7 Powder metallurgy

Powder metallurgy produces parts by compacting metal powders and sintering them at high temperature.

6. Steam Boilers – Classification and Types

6.1 Meaning of a boiler

A steam boiler is a closed pressure vessel in which water is heated by the combustion of fuel to produce steam. The steam generated is used for power generation, heating, industrial processes and mechanical work.

6.2 Classification of boilers

Boilers are classified based on several criteria:

Based on water and gas flow

  • Fire tube boilers: Hot gases pass through tubes and water surrounds the tubes. Suitable for low pressure and small capacity.
  • Water tube boilers: Water flows inside tubes and hot gases surround the tubes. Suitable for high pressure and large capacity.

Based on position of furnace

  • Internally fired: Furnace is inside the boiler shell.
  • Externally fired: Furnace is outside the boiler shell.

Based on mobility

  • Stationary boilers: Used in power plants and industries.
  • Portable boilers: Used for temporary or mobile applications.

6.3 Important types of boilers

Cochran Boiler

A vertical, multi‑tubular, fire‑tube boiler. Compact, simple and used for small power and process applications.

Lancashire Boiler

A horizontal, internally fired, fire‑tube boiler with two large flue tubes. Used for medium pressure steam generation.

Babcock & Wilcox Boiler

A horizontal, externally fired, water‑tube boiler. Capable of generating high pressure steam and widely used in power plants.

7. Steam Turbines – Introduction and Classification

7.1 Meaning

A steam turbine is a rotary prime mover that converts the energy of high‑pressure steam into mechanical work. The steam expands through nozzles or blades, producing high velocity jets that rotate the turbine shaft.

7.2 Working principle

Steam enters the turbine at high pressure and velocity. As it flows over the blades, it loses energy, which is transferred to the rotor in the form of mechanical rotation. This rotation drives a generator to produce electricity.

7.3 Classification of steam turbines

Based on energy conversion

  • Impulse turbine: Steam expands completely in the nozzle. Blades only change the direction of steam flow. Pressure drop occurs mainly in the nozzle.
  • Reaction turbine: Steam expands partly in fixed blades and partly in moving blades. Pressure drop occurs in both, producing reaction force.

Based on steam flow

  • Axial flow turbine
  • Radial flow turbine

8. Power Plants – Introduction and Classification

8.1 Meaning

A power plant is an industrial facility where energy is converted into electrical energy on a large scale. It consists of a prime mover, generator, fuel system, cooling system and various auxiliary units.

8.2 Classification of power plants

Based on energy source

  • Thermal power plant: Uses coal, oil or gas. Heat from combustion produces steam which drives a steam turbine.
  • Hydroelectric power plant: Uses potential energy of water stored at height.
  • Nuclear power plant: Uses nuclear fission to generate heat.
  • Solar power plant: Uses solar radiation (PV or solar thermal).
  • Wind power plant: Uses kinetic energy of wind to rotate turbines.

Based on prime mover

  • Steam turbine power plant
  • Gas turbine power plant
  • Diesel engine power plant
