1. Organic Reaction Mechanisms

1.1 Substitution Reactions

Substitution involves replacement of a leaving group (LG) by a nucleophile (Nu) or electrophile. Deep theory: Rate-determining step (RDS) dictates kinetics; stereochemistry reflects ion-pairing and solvent effects (protic vs. aprotic). Hammett σ constants correlate substituent effects in aromatic systems.

1.1.1 SN1 Mechanism

Unimolecular nucleophilic substitution: Two-step, carbocation intermediate (rate = k[RX]). Favored by 3° alkyl halides, polar protic solvents (stabilize ions via H-bonding), weak Nu. Deep theory: Ion-pair collapse (tight vs. solvent-separated) leads to racemization (50% inversion/retention); anchimeric assistance (neighboring group participation) accelerates via cyclic intermediates. E1 side product via deprotonation.

R₃C-X → R₃C⁺ + X⁻ (slow) → R₃C-Nu + X⁻ (fast)

1.1.2 SN2 Mechanism

Bimolecular: Concerted, backside attack (inversion of config.), rate = k[RX][Nu]. Favored by 1°/methyl, strong Nu (CN⁻, I⁻), aprotic solvents (reduce ion solvation). Deep theory: Transition state (TS) charge dispersal; β-branching sterics increase Ea (A-values: tBu 4.9 kcal/mol). No rearrangements.

1.1.3 Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS) in Benzene

EAS: Electrophile (E⁺) attacks π-system, forming arenium ion (σ-complex, Wheland intermediate). Deep theory: Addition-elimination; RDS is formation (Ea ~20-30 kcal/mol) or deprotonation based on E⁺ stability. Activating groups (NH₂, OR) ortho/para direct via resonance donation; deactivators (NO₂, CN) meta direct via inductive withdrawal. Hammett: log(k/k_H) = ρ σ (ρ <0 activating).

Ar-H + E⁺ → [Ar-H-E]⁺ → Ar-E + H⁺

1.1.4 Free Radical Halogenation of Alkanes

Chain reaction: Initiation (hv or Δ: X₂ → 2X•), Propagation (X• + RH → HX + R•; R• + X₂ → RX + X•), Termination (2R• → R-R). Deep theory: Selectivity via relative rate (Cl₂: 5:4:1 1°:2°:3°; Br₂: 1:82:1600); RDS abstraction (BDE: 3° C-H 91 kcal/mol vs. 1° 101). Anti-Markovnikov in presence of peroxides for HBr.

1.2 Elimination Reactions

Elimination forms π-bonds by loss of LG + H (E1/E2). Deep theory: Zaitsev's rule (more sub. alkene favored); anti-periplanar geometry in E2. Base strength and solvent polarity dictate mechanism.

1.2.1 Elimination in Alkyl Halides

E2: Bimolecular, concerted, anti elimination (rate = k[RX][B:⁻]), strong base (OH⁻ in EtOH), 2°/3° RX. Deep theory: TS staggered, β-H abstraction; Hofmann product with bulky base (tBuO⁻). E1: Unimolecular, carbocation (rate = k[RX]), weak base, protic solvent; rearrangements possible.

E2: RX + B:⁻ → Alkene + HB⁺ + X⁻

1.2.2 Dehydration of Alcohols

Acid-catalyzed (H₂SO₄, 140-180°C): Protonate OH → H₂O LG → E1-like carbocation → deprotonate. Deep theory: 3° > 2° > 1° reactivity (carbocation stability); rearrangements (hydride/Me shift) to more stable cation. Ether formation side rxn at lower T (<140°C).

1.3 Addition Reactions

Addition across π-bonds. Deep theory: Markovnikov regioselectivity (Nu to less sub. C); orbital symmetry (FMO theory) for pericyclic.

1.3.1 Electrophilic Addition in Alkenes

Two-step: π-attack by E⁺ → carbocation → Nu⁻. Deep theory: Bridged ions (halonium) in Br₂/Cl₂ prevent rearrangements; HBr peroxides → anti-Markov. (free radical). Rate: k[alkene][E⁺].

R-CH=CH₂ + H⁺ → R-CH⁺-CH₃ → R-CH₂-CH₂-X

1.3.2 Free Radical Addition in Alkenes

Chain: Initiation (peroxide RO•), Propagation (RO• + HBr → ROH + Br•; Br• + alkene → bromoalkyl•; bromoalkyl• + HBr → prod + Br•). Deep theory: Low selectivity (Br• abstracts H slowly); used for HBr only.

1.3.3 Nucleophilic Addition in Aldehydes and Ketones

Carbonyl C electrophilic (δ⁺); Nu attacks → tetrahedral intermediate → protonation. Deep theory: Aldehydes > ketones (sterics); Grignard (RMgBr) → alcohols. Aldol: Enolate + carbonyl (self-condensation).

R₂C=O + Nu:⁻ → R₂C(O⁻)-Nu → R₂C(OH)-Nu

1.4 Rearrangement Reactions

Skeletal shifts in intermediates. Deep theory: Migratory aptitude (H > Ph > 3° alkyl > 2° > 1° > Me); driven by stability gain.

1.4.1 Carbocation Rearrangements

1,2-shifts in SN1/E1: Hydride (1,2-H shift) or alkyl to adjacent δ⁺ C. Deep theory: Wagner-Meerwein in terpenes; pinacol → pinacolone (Me migration).

CH₃-CH₂-CH⁺-CH₃ → CH₃-CH⁺-CH-CH₃ (hydride shift)

1.4.2 Free Radical Rearrangements

Rare, but 1,2-shifts in alkyl radicals (e.g., cyclopropylmethyl → but-3-enyl, k~10⁶ s⁻¹). Deep theory: Orbital overlap in TS; less common than ionic due to lower polarity.

2. Drugs

2.1 Introduction

Drugs are chemical agents modulating physiological functions via receptor binding or enzyme inhibition. Deep theory: SAR (structure-activity relationship) guides design; pharmacokinetics (ADME: absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion). Analgesics like NSAIDs inhibit COX (cyclooxygenase) reducing prostaglandins (pain/inflammation mediators).

2.2 Aspirin (Acetylsalicylic Acid)

Synthesis: Acetylation of salicylic acid (from phenol carboxylation or Kolbe-Schmitt). Deep theory: Acetic anhydride + salicylic acid (H₂SO₄ catalyst) → ester + AcOH; yield 90-95%, mp 135°C.

C₆H₄(OH)COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₄(OCOCH₃)COOH + CH₃COOH

Properties: White crystalline, soluble in EtOH/alkali (not H₂O), pKa 3.5 (carboxyl), hydrolyzes to salicylic acid in vivo.

Uses: Analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory (400-1000 mg/dose); antiplatelet (irreversible COX-1 acetylation, 81 mg prophylactic); gastric irritation risk.

2.3 Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)

Synthesis: From p-aminophenol + acetic anhydride (or phenol nitration/reduction/acetylation). Deep theory: Selective COX-2 inhibition in CNS (not peripheral); hepatic metabolism via CYP450 to NAPQI (toxic, detox by GSH).

H₂N-C₆H₄-OH + (CH₃CO)₂O → CH₃CONH-C₆H₄-OH + CH₃COOH

Properties: White powder, mp 169°C, soluble in hot H₂O/EtOH, pKa 9.5 (phenol), non-acidic (no GI bleed).

Uses: Analgesic/antipyretic (500-1000 mg/dose, max 4g/day); safer than aspirin for ulcers; hepatotoxicity overdose (>10g).
