Unit 3 – Corrosion and its Control
Definition, Mechanisms, Types, Protection Methods

1. Definition and Significance of Corrosion

1.1 Definition

Corrosion is the deterioration of a material (usually metal) by reaction with its environment, leading to loss of properties like strength or appearance. Deep theory: It's an electrochemical process where metal atoms lose electrons (oxidation) and form compounds (e.g., rust: 4Fe + 3O₂ + 6H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃). Uniform or localized, influenced by pH, O₂, ions.

1.2 Significance

Economic: Global cost ~3-4% GDP ($2.5T/year); safety (bridges, pipelines fail); environmental (leachates). Deep theory: Corrosion fatigue reduces life 10-100x; preventive design saves 20-50% lifecycle cost. Industries: Marine (ships), Oil&Gas (pipelines), Construction (rebar).

  • Factors: Temperature (doubles rate/10°C), Humidity (>60% RH accelerates), Pollutants (SO₂ forms H₂SO₄).
Corrosion Rate (CR, mm/y) = (K × W) / (A × T × ρ) (K=const., W=wt. loss g, A=area cm², T=time h, ρ=density g/cm³)

2. Mechanisms of Corrosion

2.1 Chemical (Dry) Corrosion

Direct reaction with environment without water (e.g., high temp. gases). Deep theory: Oxidation (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, Pilling-Bedworth ratio >1 for protective scale), Halogenation (Zn + Cl₂ → ZnCl₂, non-protective). Thermogravimetric analysis shows parabolic growth kinetics: x² = k t (x=oxide thickness).

  • Types: Atmospheric (rusting), High-temp (furnaces).

2.2 Electrochemical (Wet) Corrosion

Involves electrolyte; anodic (M → Mⁿ⁺ + n e⁻) and cathodic (O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻) reactions. Deep theory: Mixed potential theory (Evans diagram: intersection of anodic/cathodic lines); Pourbaix diagram maps E vs. pH stability. Galvanic series ranks nobility (Au > SS > Cu > Fe > Zn > Mg).

Nernst Equation: E = E° - (RT/nF) ln Q (predicts potential, R=8.314 J/molK, F=96485 C/mol)
Faraday's Law: m = (I t M) / (n F) (mass loss m g, I=current A, t=s, M=mol wt.)

3. Types of Corrosion

3.1 Galvanic Corrosion

Dissimilar metals in contact form galvanic cell; less noble (anode) corrodes faster. Deep theory: Potential difference >0.15V accelerates; area ratio (cathode:anode >1) worsens. E.g., Zn-Fe in seawater (Zn sacrifices). Prevent by insulators, similar metals.

3.2 Concentration Corrosion

Differential aeration/concentration cells; O₂-rich areas cathodic. Deep theory: Evans' oxygen concentration cell; under deposits, crevices create local anodes (pH drops via hydrolysis). Rate ∝ ∇C (concentration gradient).

  • Examples: Rust under paint, soil corrosion (pipes).

3.3 Pitting Corrosion

Localized attack forming pits; autocatalytic (Cl⁻ breaks passive film, FeCl₂ hydrolyzes → HCl). Deep theory: Pit growth: Initiation (film breakdown), Propagation (i_crit > i_pass); Pitting Potential E_pit vs. protection potential E_prot (E_pit - E_corr >0.3V vulnerable). SS 316L resistant (Mo stabilizes).

Pit Depth Rate = K × exp(-Q/RT) (Arrhenius, Q=activation energy)

4. Protection from Corrosion

4.1 Protective Coatings

Barrier layers prevent contact. Deep theory: Porosity <1% ideal; cathodic (Zn) vs. anodic (Al) protection if breached.

  • Galvanization: Zn coating (hot-dip 50-150 μm); Zn corrodes preferentially (E_Zn = -0.76V vs. Fe -0.44V). Sacrificial, self-heals scratches.
  • Tinning: Sn coating (electrolytic 5-20 μm); anodic to food cans (Sn-Fe contact safe), but cathodic to steel (pits if breached).

4.2 Cathodic Protection

Makes structure cathode: External current (rectifiers, 10-100 mA/m²) or sacrificial anodes. Deep theory: Potential shift to -0.85V vs. SCE for steel (Tafel kinetics: overpotential η = a + b log i).

  • Sacrificial Anode: Active metal (Mg -1.5V, Al-Zn -1.05V) attached; anode dissolves (current I = (E_c - E_a)/R_p). Life = (Wt. anode × Util. factor) / (Current density × Eq. wt. / F).

4.3 Modifications in Design

Avoid crevices, use alloys (SS), insulation. Deep theory: Stress corrosion cracking (SCC) via H embrittlement; design for drainage, uniform thickness. Cathodic interference avoided by backfill.

Anode Life (y) = (m × 8760) / (I × t × n × F / (26.8 × Eq. wt.)) (m=mass kg, I=A, t=thickness m)
